NSE Swing Label Study

What should a 5 to 20 day swing trader on NSE train a model on? A team of research agents rebuilt 5.75 years of NSE data from the exchange's own files, tested about 2,000 label and portfolio variants over three rounds, and then tried to break the winner. This page reports what survived, what it can realistically earn, and where it fails.

Train on a 20-day relative label: rank each stock's market-neutral return against every other stock that day. Do not train on "will it go up?".

For each stock on each day, the label takes the return from the next morning's open to the close 20 trading days later. It removes what the stock's market beta, volatility, size and 12-month momentum already explain, and ranks the remainder against all eligible stocks that day. The top 30% are +1 (will beat their peers), the bottom 30% are −1 (will lag them) and the middle 40% are 0.

Why this one. It is the only label whose event rate does not depend on the market: exactly 30 / 40 / 30 on every day, in bull and bear markets, for large and small stocks. Every "will it go up?" label we built calls about 13% of stocks "up" on a weak day and 83% on a strong one, and moves 0.71–0.88 with the market, so a model trained on it learns market timing, and none of our timing models could time this market. The relative label also fixes the existing label's defects: an entry price you can actually trade, a blank instead of a zero when the outcome is unknown, and adjusted prices.

Up and down. Buy from the top of the score. Use the bottom 30% as a never-buy and exit list. Do not short it: those stocks lag their peers but rarely fall, and every short-selling version lost money.

What it earned. On 2023–2025, where every choice was made, a weekly book of the top 20 made 30.3% a year against 12.0% for random picks (an average daily excess worth 15.3% a year, t 4.0). In the 2026 holdout it made 10.2% against 10.2%: no edge, although the model's ranking skill held up and the avoid list kept working.

What to plan on. The final examiner's verdict is alive but unproven. After correcting for the roughly 2,000 variants tried and weighing in 2026, the best estimate of the edge is about +4.4% a year over random picks (+6.9% over the equal-weight market), with a 90% chance that it is positive, and drawdowns as deep as the small- and mid-cap market's (−27% in early 2025). Keep it under about ₹20 crore, trade it small first, and stop under the pre-set kill rule.

The one idea to carry away: direction and selection are different questions. "Will the market rise?" is a timing decision; "which stocks will beat the others?" is a selection decision. A label that mixes them is mostly a market label. Keep the label relative and make the market decision separately, once, as a fixed level of exposure.

Why "will it go up?" labels fail at 5 to 20 days

A label is useful only if it describes the stock. Every absolute label we built (the repo's current label, a clean direction label, cost-aware classes, triple barriers) mostly describes the market instead. The chart shows, for each month, the share of eligible stocks that each label calls "up" over the next 20 trading days, against the market's own move over the same 20 days.

The relative label stays at 30% every month (correlation 0.03). A model trained on the direction label spends its effort learning the bars, which is market timing.

Are the labels right? Eight definitions side by side

Twenty-day horizon, tradable universe, every trading day of 2021–2026. "Weak day" and "strong day" are the 5th and 95th percentile of the daily up-share. The correlation column compares each day's up-share with the Nifty 500's return over the same 20 days: near 1 means the label is the market, near 0 means it is about the stock. Net returns buy at the next open, sell 20 days later and pay 0.5%.

An absolute label's "up" rows made money by construction (the class is defined by the outcome), so the money columns say nothing about predictability. What matters is the swing column and the correlation column. Every absolute label swings from roughly 10–15% "up" on weak days to 75–83% on strong days and correlates 0.71–0.88 with the market. The dual label (relative AND absolute) inherits that dependence: its correlation is 0.70.

The same comparison at 5, 10, 15 and 20 days

Longer horizons do not make absolute labels less market-driven: the same-window correlation stays at 0.85–0.88 for the sign labels at every horizon. The relative labels stay at exactly 30% up with near-zero correlation at every horizon.

The repo's current label, defect by defect

Measured on the tradable universe, 2021–2026, within each company's own price history (kinder than the repo's ticker-keyed shift). The disagreement with the clean label is only 3–6% of rows, but it is concentrated where it matters: unknown futures counted as "not up", split and bonus days counted as crashes (4,261 fake crashes at 20 days), and a close-entry return that nobody can trade.

Three more pieces of evidence that direction labels learn the market

  • The shuffle canary. Shuffling a direction label across stocks within each day destroys all stock information and keeps only the day's base rate. A model trained on the shuffled label scored a pooled AUC of 0.52–0.54, almost the same as the real model. Pooled accuracy on these labels measures timing. Measured stock by stock within each day, the real models reach only about 0.53.
  • The 2026 holdout. 2026 was a falling market (Nifty 500 −7.8% to 14 Oct). Every absolute-label book failed or tied random selection there: the clean direction label made 3.5% a year against 6.9% for random picks; the triple barrier made 6.6% against 6.9%.
  • The repo's current label adds three defects at these horizons. It enters at the signal day's close, a price you cannot trade after seeing that close; the overnight gap it hands the model is worth about 26 bps per trade at every horizon (the 5-day outcome averages 35.7 bps from the close but 10.0 bps from the next open). It turns missing outcomes into "down": 3.8% of its 20-day "not up" class is really "unknown", and in the final month of data every stock is "down". And by default it runs on unadjusted prices, which creates 4,261 fake 20-day crashes on split and bonus days.

A swing trader pays the round-trip cost on every trade, so a shorter hold pays it more often. The overnight tax

Indian stock returns arrive almost entirely between one day's close and the next day's open. During trading sessions the average stock drifted down. A label computed at the close can only be acted on at the next open, after that night's gap has already been paid, so every new position gives up one overnight gap.

Where the returns happened: overnight gaps vs trading sessions
Growth of ₹1 in the equal-weight tradable universe from January 2023, earning only the close-to-open gaps, only the open-to-close sessions, or both. Before costs; an illustration, not a strategy.
Log scale. Overnight-only grew ₹1 to about ₹10.0; session-only shrank it to ₹0.21; holding throughout gave ₹2.01. The pattern holds in every liquidity third (least liquid +117% a year overnight, most liquid +71%, 2023–25), so it is not only an illiquid-stock effect. Part of it may come from how NSE forms its opening (auction) and closing (last-30-minute average) prices.

The gap given up is the same per trade at every horizon (about 0.27% in 2023–25, 0.18% in 2026), so it hurts short holds most. A 5-day trader gives up about 60% of what a close-entry label promises; a 20-day trader about 15%. Together with costs, this is why 5-day swing trading on next-open entries is a losing game on NSE, whatever the label.

Skill points the other way. The relative label at 10 days carries the most raw skill (phase-averaged excess t 4.8 on 2023–2025) but trades twice as often. At 80 bps the 10-day book made 16.2% a year against 25.2% for the 20-day book, and at 130 bps only 2.6%. The 15-day book sits between them on every measure. We chose 20 days because its edge survives realistic costs. The training horizon itself matters little: models trained on 10, 15 and 20-day labels and all held for 20 days earned +18.1%, +15.1% and +18.0% a year over random on 2023–2025.

Filters and conditions: what "both must accept" really does

In the label review you suggested labelling a stock only when conditions accept. A condition can sit in two places, and they behave very differently.

On the sample (input side)

"Only consider stocks that pass the condition." Eligibility filters and setup gates decide which stock-days exist as training rows and as candidates. They never change a label's value.

Use them to define what is tradable: EQ series, price ≥ ₹20, 20-day median turnover ≥ ₹2 crore, listed ≥ 120 days, not circuit-locked, ATR 0.5–15%, equity ISIN. Only 42.5% of stock-days pass.

On the label (output side)

"Call it +1 only if it beat its peers AND rose." This puts the market back into the label: whether a stock rose over 20 days depends mostly on the market.

Avoid them. The dual label's +1 share is about 30% when the market rises over the 20 days, 26% when it falls by up to 5% and 13% when it falls by more.

Filters move the relative event rates by at most about 5 points. The trend gate raises both tails (33.3% up and 33.1% down), so it adds volatile names rather than winners. Inside every filter the relative classes still separate winners from losers by 19–24 points of 20-day return, and about 90% of +1 stocks made money after costs. Filters can narrow the universe without breaking the label.

The winning label: a 20-day relative rank, neutral to market, size, volatility and momentum

The label every later result uses. Each step exists to remove one known source of bias.

  1. Outcome. r = close[t+20] / open[t+1] − 1, on prices adjusted for splits, bonuses, rights and dividends using NSE's own book-closure records. Left blank when the stock cannot be traded through the window (suspension, a corporate action we could not adjust, a data gap), never set to zero.
  2. Universe. Stocks eligible on day t: EQ series, price ≥ ₹20, 20-day median turnover ≥ ₹2 crore, listed ≥ 120 days, not circuit-locked, ATR between 0.5% and 15%, an equity ISIN (ETFs and fund units excluded).
  3. Trim outliers. Winsorise r at that day's 1st and 99th percentiles.
  4. Remove what is already known. Regress r across that day's stocks on shrunk market beta (0.5 × 60-day beta + 0.5), 20-day volatility, log turnover (size and liquidity) and 12-1 month momentum.
  5. Rank the remainder. The residual is the part of the return those four cannot explain. Rank it against all eligible stocks that day, from 0 to 1. The model is trained on this rank.
  6. Classes for reporting. +1 above 0.70 (beat peers), −1 at or below 0.30 (lagged peers), 0 in between.

Among F&O stocks the −1 class is under-represented (25.5% instead of 30%), because the largest and most liquid stocks rarely land in the bottom of the cross-section. Anyone who trades only F&O names should rank inside that universe instead of reusing the full-universe classes.

What the classes are worth

Over 2021–2026, a stock labelled +1 returned +12.2% net over its 20 days on average and 90.4% of them made money after costs; a −1 stock returned −9.3%. Those are outcomes, not forecasts: they show the classes mean something in rupees. They also depend on the market. When the Nifty 500 fell more than 5% over the 20 days, +1 stocks averaged only +0.8% and fewer than half made money (see Scenarios). A relative label tells you which stocks will do better than the others, not whether they will rise.

Can a model learn it?

Yes, modestly and consistently. The production score (an average of 10 LightGBM models, described under How to invest) ranks stocks with an out-of-sample rank correlation (IC) of 0.066 on 2023–2025 (t 9.0) and 0.063 in 2026 (t 4.8), with no decay into the holdout. The model is better at spotting laggards than leaders: its AUC for the bottom 30% is 0.550 on 2023–25 (t 11.3) and 0.556 in 2026 (t 6.4), against 0.517 (t 3.7) and 0.508 (t 1.8) for the top 30%. That asymmetry is why the avoid list is the most durable part of the process.

What went wrong in 2026

The model still ranked stocks in 2026: its IC held at 0.063 and the bottom-30% list kept underperforming. What failed was the top of the list. Per signal day, the 20 top-ranked stocks beat the equal-weight market by +1.33% per 20-day trade on 2023–25 (t 4.2 with a conservative correction for overlapping trades) but only +0.11% in 2026 (t 0.24). Two things explain most of it. First, 2026 was a momentum year: a plain 12-1 month momentum top-20 beat random picks by 32.9% a year, and this label deliberately removes momentum, so the book was tilted towards large, recently weak stocks. Second, the model's skill was always concentrated in spotting laggards (bottom-30% AUC 0.55) rather than leaders (top-30% AUC 0.52), and in 2026 the leader side fell to near a coin flip (0.508).

Averaging ten models did not change this. The single model's 2026 excess (+3.9%) was luck within model noise: three single models scored +3.9%, +2.1% and −1.1%, and every ensemble lands near zero.

Robustness: the devil's-advocate audit

After the judges chose the label, an independent agent rebuilt it from scratch with its own code and tried to break it. Its verdict: survives, with caveats.

  • Reproduction. Its own forward windows matched the lab's with 0 mismatches over 3.0 M rows. The rebuilt rank matched the stored label to 3×10⁻⁸, and the retrained model's score correlated 0.9999999999986 with the original. No leakage and no simulator artefact were found.
  • New companies. Split into two random halves of companies, both halves beat random on 2023–25 (+10.1%, t 2.4 and +12.1%, t 3.7). A model trained on one half and scored on the other kept its IC (0.055–0.061), so it is not memorising stock names.
  • Not a simple factor tilt. Ten single-factor top-20 books (low volatility, reversal, small size, momentum, low beta, delivery, 52-week high, and a composite) earned between −11.3% and +9.3% a year over random on 2023–25. Regressed on six long-short factors, the book's alpha was +17.7% a year (t 4.5) with R² 0.05.
  • Smooth, no cliffs. Top-K from 5 to 200 names, rebalancing every 1 to 20 days, costs from 30 to 200 bps: every 2023–25 cell beat random with t between 3.1 and 5.0. Smaller books earn more (K=5: +24–26%, K=20: +16–17%, K=50: +10–11%) and are noisier.
  • Not a lottery. The whole trade distribution shifts. The median trade made +1.33% against −0.06% for random picks, and the share of trades losing more than 20% was 1.9% against 2.1%.

Where the audit found weaknesses

  • 2026 is weak. In 2026 the single model's phase-averaged excess was +3.9% a year (t 0.60). Across three LightGBM seeds it was +3.9%, +2.1% and −1.1%, and a 3-seed rank ensemble made −0.2%. About 90% of the 2026 excess came in the first quarter.
  • 2024 carried the selection window. Excess over random by year: 2023 +11.4%, 2024 +27.6%, 2025 +8.0%. Every year is positive, and without 2024 the 2023–25 excess is +9.7%.
  • The edge lives in mid and small caps. Re-ranked inside the top 200 stocks by turnover (an F&O proxy) it was only +3.6% a year on 2023–25 (t 1.2). That is also where market impact bites.
  • Single models are noisy. Two models trained with different random seeds share only 63% of their top 20 picks. Production must average several models.
  • Factor headwinds in 2026. The label neutralises momentum, and 2026 was a momentum year: a plain 12-1 momentum top-20 earned +32.9% over random. The book was tilted to large, recently weak stocks.

Scenarios: what changes when the market changes

The same 20-day labels, split by market conditions. The first nine scenarios are known on the signal day, so a trader could act on them. The last four use the market's actual next 20 days, which nobody knows in advance; they show what each label does in a crash or a rally.

Direction labels are a market forecast

Conditions known in advance barely move the direction label (44% to 58% of stocks "up"), so they cannot be used to time it. The market's actual move swings it from 14% in a crash to 78% in a rally. A model that learns the direction label is learning to forecast the market.

Relative labels don't stop a falling market

The relative label is 30% up in every row. What changes is what a +1 is worth: in a rally the top 30% averaged +20.1% net, in a crash only +0.8%, and fewer than half made money while the bottom 30% lost 19.0%. Picking better stocks is a separate job from deciding how much to hold.

2021–2026 holds only about 64 independent 20-day periods, so these splits describe the past; they are not trading rules.

The tradable version: the production score in each scenario

Per signal day: buy the 20 top-ranked stocks at the next open, sell 20 days later, pay 0.5%, and compare with every eligible stock bought the same way. The avoid tail is the bottom 30% of the score.

On 2023–2025 the top 20 beat the market in every condition known in advance (each with t above 2.5, even after a conservative correction for overlapping trades), and the avoid tail lagged in every one. In 2026 the top-20 edge was close to zero in almost every condition, while the avoid tail still lagged in most of them. The one exception was 2026's sharp rebound from mid-March to early April, when the avoid tail beat the market by 1.7% per trade: a rally led by beaten-down, volatile stocks, the names a momentum-neutral label ranks lowest. On the strong-rally days of 2023–25 the avoid tail kept lagging (−0.47%, t −3.9), so this was one episode rather than a rule.

Up and down: what to do with the −1 side

You asked for a label where "up is fine and down is fine". The label has both ends: +1 for stocks that will beat their peers and −1 for stocks that will lag them. Both ends carry real information. Whether each end can be turned into money is a separate question, and the answer differs.

These are realised classes, known only after 20 days. They show the label means something in rupees at both ends: 91% of +1 stocks made money after costs, and 71–76% of −1 stocks lost more than 5%.

Every way of using the down side, tested

Ten pre-declared strategies, each simulated daily with Indian costs, margin and futures carry: index futures hedges, F&O long/short books, a short overlay and a short-only book. Chosen on 2023–2025; 2026 reported only.

"Gain" is the yearly difference against the unhedged long book (for the avoid veto, against the equal-weight market), with its t-statistic. Adoption needed t ≥ 2 on 2023–25 without a drawdown more than 3 points worse. 2026 figures are annualised over the 185 trading days to 7 October. Sharpe is the plain return-to-volatility ratio; measured over the cash rate, the hedged books score lower than the unhedged one (1.05 and 0.95 against 1.15). Futures shorts earn the cash rate as carry (Nifty 1D Rate) and pay 25 bps per round trip; margin is 15% for index futures and 25% for stock futures, half held in cash.

Use −1 as a veto and exit list

Never buy a stock in the bottom 30% of the score; if you hold one for other reasons, treat it as an exit candidate. As a stand-alone screen this added +3.4% a year over the equal-weight market on 2023–25 (t 3.5) and +1.5% in 2026. It is not just "avoid volatile stocks": a plain high-volatility veto earned +0.3% (2023–25) and −3.5% (2026), and after controlling for volatility, beta, momentum and liquidity at once, the avoid list still kept about 3% a year of stock-specific value in both windows (+2.9% on 2023–25, t 5.2; +3.1% in 2026, t 5.9).

Don't short the −1 stocks

The bottom decile barely falls, and shorting fights India's upward drift: a random F&O short book returned −11.5% a year on 2023–25, 17 points behind cash. The model's shorts beat random shorts on 2023–25 (+8.8% a year, t 1.3) and lost to them badly in 2026 (−17.9%). Inside the F&O list the score is weak (top minus bottom +0.9% per trade, t 1.4) and in 2026 the bottom F&O decile rose the most.

An index hedge is a separate tool: it does not use the −1 side at all. Shorting Nifty 50 futures against the book (ex-ante beta) cut the 2023–25 maximum drawdown from −26.8% to −17.0% and its correlation with the market from 0.80 to 0.20, but it gave up about 9 points of annual return, because the hedge also gives up the market's rise. In 2026 the same hedge returned 34.9% a year, a windfall from large caps falling while the book's mid and small caps held up. That is one regime, not a forecast. Use a hedge as a dial for how much market risk you want, not as a source of return.

How to invest with it

The process below is the frozen baseline plus the one execution change that passed the pre-registered test. Each rule traces back to evidence on this page. Anything not listed was tested and rejected.

  1. After every close, rebuild and score. Load the day's NSE bhavcopy, delivery file, index file and book-closure records, adjust prices, recompute the 52 features, and score every eligible stock with the production score (the average of ten models' daily ranks). Have the scores ready before the next morning's pre-open session: each day of delay costs about 1.9 points a year.
  2. Mark the avoid list. The bottom 30% of today's score. Never buy these names. If you hold one for any other reason, treat it as an exit candidate. Never short it.
  3. Once a week, on a fixed weekday, rotate one sleeve. The book has four sleeves of 20 names, and each week one sleeve is due. In the next morning's opening session, sell its names that are no longer in that evening's top 20, buy the new top-20 names, and leave untouched the names that are still in the top 20. Each name is held for at least 20 trading days. The results on this page average all five weekdays; any single weekday adds luck worth several points a year in either direction (−2.8% to +8.1% in 2026). If your operations allow it, renewing a twentieth of the book every trading day removes that luck.
  4. Size equally. 1/80 of capital per name (1/20 of a sleeve), capped at 5% of the stock's 20-day median traded value. Anything above the cap waits in a liquid fund.
  5. Hold for 20 trading days. No stop-losses, profit targets or trailing stops. Exit early only when forced by a suspension or a corporate action that cannot be adjusted (demergers, schemes of arrangement).
  6. Choose market exposure once, not every week. 100% invested is the default. If the drawdowns below are too deep for you, hold a fixed 80% or 60% with the rest in a liquid fund. Do not time the market with the 200-day average, VIX or breadth.
  7. Retrain once a year with the same recipe: ten LightGBM models (five seeds × two halves of the training dates), an expanding window, a 22-day embargo, ranks averaged per day.
  8. Check the dashboard every week and apply the review and kill rules in What to expect.

Why trade at the open and keep what still qualifies

The frozen book sold every position at the close of its 20th day and bought the replacements at the next open, so it was out of the market every rebalance night. Holding through that night, trading only in the next opening session and keeping names that are still in the top 20 added +3.8% a year on 2023–25 (paired t 12.7) and +2.1% in 2026, with no change in drawdown. It also cut orders from about 39 to 36 a week. Random picks gain +3.2% a year from the same timing, and 83% of the gain comes simply from selling at the next open instead of the close, so this is the Indian overnight premium, not stock-picking skill. Measured against a random book traded the same way, the skill is unchanged: +15.9% a year on 2023–25 and +0.4% in 2026. The rule was added after the first carry rule turned out to decide at the close using that same close's signal (not executable). It passes the adoption test's thresholds but was not on the pre-declared list, so treat it as an execution convention, not a finding. The overnight premium also shrank from 0.34% a night in 2024 to 0.13% in 2026, so treat the gain as uncertain and watch your opening fills.

Every add-on, judged by the pre-registered rule

An add-on was adopted only if it was declared in advance (or disclosed as an extra), could be traded as specified, improved the 2023–25 book with a paired t of at least 2, and did not deepen the worst fall by more than 3 points. Twenty-one were judged.

Positive "change in worst fall" means a shallower drawdown. Several rejected ideas cut drawdowns (index hedges, early exits, lower exposure) but gave up return; they remain available as risk dials, not as edges. The avoid veto is not an add-on to the top-20 book, which never holds bottom-30% names; it is the avoid list already in the process.

Costs and tax

Turnover is high: about 12.6 round trips a year per slot, or 986 a year for the whole book. At 50 bps that is a cost drag of about 6.2% a year (5.7% with the carry), already inside every return on this page. Slippage beyond about 14 bps per side against the official open uses up the 50 bps budget, and at the 80 bps stress level 2023–25 returns fall by 4.7 points a year. All gains are short-term for an individual, and securities transaction tax is not deductible from capital gains, so tax is due even in flat years.

What to expect, and when to stop

The planning number is an edge of about +4.4% a year over random picks (90% range −1.1% to +9.9%, a 90% chance it is positive), or +6.9% over the equal-weight market, not the +15% seen on 2023–25. It is the 2023–25 result after the multiple-testing haircut (+5.7%), weighed by precision together with a 2026 exam that showed almost no edge. The ranges below were simulated at a round +5%, close enough to the same picture.

In plain numbers, before tax and after 0.5% costs: if the market repeats 2023–25 (equal-weight market +11.2% a year), the book would make about 16–18% a year; in a year like 2026 (+8.6%), about 13–16%; in a period like 2021–22 (−2.5%), about 2–4%. Selling at the next open adds the overnight premium on top (+3.2% a year on 2023–25, +2.1% in 2026, shrinking). Short-term capital-gains tax, including cess, takes about 4.2% a year (5.0% with the open-session execution).

Even with a real +5% edge, about one year in four ends behind random picks, and a strategy with no edge at all looks like a +5% one about one year in four. One year of live results cannot tell a working book from a dead one. That is why the stop rules below combine money with signal quality. These ranges are for a book that staggers its entries across the week; a book that always buys on the same weekday is a little noisier (about a 26% chance of a year behind random).

Total return = the equal-weight market plus the edge, resampled together in 40-day blocks of 2023–25 history. The market part keeps its 2023–25 average (11.2% a year after costs), which may be optimistic. The edge moves the middle of the range; market risk sets its width.

Review and kill rules

Review (investigate, no automatic action)

Any one of: the 6-month lead over random below −5%; the 3-month average IC below 0.02; the 12-month lead over random below zero. Expect at least one review in two years even for a good book (72% chance).

Kill (stop new entries, let sleeves run off)

Only when the 12-month lead over random is below zero and the 3-month IC is below 0.02 at the same weekly check. False-alarm rate 7.6% within 24 months for a good book; it catches a dead strategy 85.5% of the time, typically after about a year.

On the backtest, only the 6-month review fired, in January 2024, and it was a false alarm: the 12-month lead never fell below 6.6%. No rule fired in 2026. At the last check (30 Sep 2026) the 12-month lead was +7.8%, but it still contains the strong final quarter of 2025, so a review is likely in early 2027 unless new excess appears.

The forward test: frozen before any new data arrives

2026 was reported in earlier rounds, so the only clean exam is data after 14 October 2026. The protocol is fixed now:

  1. Frozen specification. The label recipe, the ten-model production score, the four-sleeve top-20 book with open-session execution, 50 bps costs (80 bps stress), the ₹2 crore liquidity universe, the bottom-30% avoid list and 100% exposure. No hedge, no market-timing layer, no event sleeve.
  2. Clock. Signals from 15 October 2026 count as fresh. Every week the lab recomputes the paper book (all five weekdays averaged), the open-session version, random books with 25 seeds under both timings, the equal-weight market and the Nifty 500 with a dividend proxy. The live account, on its one weekday, is reported alongside.
  3. Retraining. Once a year in the first week of January, starting January 2027, with the identical recipe: the same ten models, features, parameters and label, trained on everything up to 22 trading days before 1 January.
  4. Success test. At each annual review, the fresh lead of the paper book over 25-seed random picks. It counts as confirmed when the one-sided t reaches 1.645. The plan is rejected if the fresh lead falls below the planning number by more than its combined uncertainty (about −5.5% a year after 24 months); the planning number is then updated, and an updated estimate at or below zero stops the strategy.
  5. Review and kill. The rules above, plus two operating checks: review if 3-month average slippage against the official open exceeds 13.85 bps per side and stop adding capital above 28.85 bps; and if the 6-month average overnight move of the names being sold turns negative, go back to selling at the close.
  6. Challengers in the shadows. A single model, the 3-model average, the weekly avoid-list exit and the ₹5 crore universe are tracked on fresh data only. A challenger replaces the production process only after at least 24 months, with a paired t of at least 2.24 (adjusted for four challengers) and a worst fall no more than 3 points deeper.

Patience is required: with a true edge of +4.4% a year, fresh data alone confirms it with only a 15% chance after 12 months and 22% after 24. The kill rule, not the confirmation test, is what protects the money from a dead strategy. A review is likely by March 2027 (64% chance even with the planned edge), because the strong final quarter of 2025 will drop out of the 12-month window.

How much to trust it

Testing about 2,000 variants on three years of data guarantees that some look good by luck. These are the corrections we applied, and what is left after them.

The usual bar is 0.95. The family-level finding (factor-neutral relative labels beat random at 20 days) clears it easily: 73% of 429 relative specifications had t > 2 on 2023–25. The exact specification does not clear it unless only the correlation-adjusted count of about 71 independent tries is used. A Bonferroni haircut at N = 1,991 cuts the 2023–25 excess from +15.8% to +6.7% a year.

  • Phase luck. A book that buys on one fixed weekday is exposed to which weekday that is. Across the five possible weekly calendars the 2026 excess ranged from −2.0% to +9.2%. Every headline number here is the average over all five (equivalent to staggering entries daily).
  • Model noise is as large as the differences between label variants. Two labels that agree on 99.5% of rows produced books 1–4 points a year apart. 2023–25 data cannot tell the rank, 20/60/20 and top-30% encodings apart; the rank won on simplicity, lowest factor loading and best IC.
  • The holdout is lightly used. Round 1 reported 2026 numbers, which shaped round 2's focus on relative labels. 2026 is therefore a confirmation, not a clean exam. The clean exam is data after 14 Oct 2026, under the frozen protocol in What to expect.
  • Small effective sample. 2023–25 holds only about 37 independent 20-day market windows. Year-level uncertainty is larger than a Newey-West t suggests.
  • Selection statistics are weak predictors. Across 22 candidates, the 2023–25 t-statistic predicted the 2026 result poorly (Spearman 0.37); the 2023–25 excess did better (0.73), and structural regime-freeness was the best predictor of holdout survival. One ensemble with the best 2023–25 t (4.37) lost 7% a year in 2026.

Data corrections made during the research

Several things we believed early on turned out to be wrong. Each correction was made before the label was chosen, and the affected results were rebuilt.

  • NSE's PREV_CLOSE is not adjusted for corporate actions. An early check treated close-to-prev-close gaps as corporate actions and found thousands. The real cause was missing weekend sessions (the Muhurat Sunday of 12 Nov 2023, the special Saturday sessions of 20 Jan, 2 Mar and 18 May 2024, and the Budget sessions of 1 Feb 2025 and 1 Feb 2026). We downloaded those sessions and rebuilt adjustments from NSE's book-closure records: 8,545 dividends, 550 of 559 splits and bonuses located on the exact price-gap day, 161 rights issues, and 457 stock-days blocked where no adjustment was possible. The earlier audit and label review were corrected.
  • Company identity follows the ISIN, not the ticker. 255 companies changed symbol and 341 ISIN changes were bridged where the symbol continued; 7 reused symbols were kept apart.
  • Sector data was a 2026 snapshot. Using it leaked future index membership into 2021–25 (+56 bps per trade that vanished in 2026). Removed.
  • ETFs and fund units were in the universe (3.5% of eligible rows). Removed.
  • A "next bar exists" eligibility flag looked ahead. Removed; the label is blank anyway when the next bar is missing.
  • The first simulator overstated Sharpe by 12–24% and understated drawdowns by 2.6–3.3 points. Replaced by a daily mark-to-market simulator, validated against a hand-calculated trade to the paisa and against foresight canaries.

Glossary

Event rateThe share of rows in each label class (for example 30% +1, 40% 0, 30% −1).
Relative labelA label that compares a stock with the other stocks on the same day, so the market's direction cancels out.
ResidualThe part of a stock's return not explained by its beta, volatility, size and momentum on that day.
ICInformation coefficient: the rank correlation between the model's score and the realised outcome across stocks on a day, averaged over days. 0.05 is useful in equities.
AUCThe probability that a randomly chosen stock from a class gets a higher score than one outside it. 0.5 is a coin flip. Measured within each day here.
Random bookThe same portfolio rules with stocks chosen at random (5 seeds). The baseline every result is compared with.
Equal-weight universeHolding every eligible stock in equal amounts on the same calendar.
ExcessThe average daily difference between the book and the random book (or the equal-weight market), times 252. It is smaller than the gap between the two compound annual returns: on 2023–25, 15.3% against a compound gap of 18.3 points.
t (Newey-West)The t-statistic of the daily excess, corrected for overlapping holding periods. Above about 2 is unlikely to be luck for a single test.
Phase-averagedThe average of the five possible weekly entry calendars, which equals a book that staggers its entries every day.
Deflated Sharpe ratioThe probability that a strategy's Sharpe ratio is above zero after allowing for how many strategies were tried.
SleeveOne quarter of the book. Each week one sleeve is sold and rebought, so every position is held for 20 trading days.
Avoid listThe bottom 30% of the score on a day. Never buy these; consider exiting them.
2023–25 / DEVThe selection window. Every choice was made on it.
2026 / holdout1 Jan – 14 Oct 2026. Reported, never used to choose.
